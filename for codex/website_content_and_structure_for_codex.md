## Main Intro Text

I am an Assistant Professor in the Division of Operations and Logistics at the UBC Sauder School of Business. I develop algorithmic and scalable optimization methods for complex operational decision problems arising in logistics, retail, and revenue management.

My research focuses on operational systems that are both practically rich and computationally challenging, with an emphasis on approximation methods, decomposition approaches, and interpretable optimization models. I am particularly interested in problems that initially appear intractable, but contain hidden structure that can be leveraged to develop tractable and rigorous solution methods.

Before joining UBC, I worked as a Research Scientist at Amazon. My research has been published in journals including Management Science, Manufacturing & Service Operations Management, and Mathematics of Operations Research.

# RESEARCH STREAM 1

## Logistics & Supply Chain Optimization

I am interested in operational systems that become computationally difficult because of scale, uncertainty, and tightly coupled decision structures. My work in logistics and supply chain management explores how these systems can be decomposed into tractable optimization problems while preserving the operational features that drive real-world complexity.

Recent projects include workforce planning, inventory optimization, and large-scale operational planning problems with complex dynamic structure.

# RESEARCH STREAM 2

## Retail & Revenue Management

My work in retail and revenue management examines how customer behavior and demand dynamics interact with operational decision-making. I study pricing, promotions, and assortment-related problems using models that combine behavioral considerations with rigorous optimization frameworks.

A recurring theme in this work is balancing operational realism with tractable decision models that remain both analytically meaningful and computationally manageable.

### Selected Work

- High-Low Promotion Policies for Peak-End Demand Models
  with K. Panchamgam and G. Perakis

# RESEARCH STREAM 3

## Approximation & Scalable Optimization Methods

Across these application domains, my research is unified by an interest in scalable optimization methods for computationally challenging operational problems. Much of my work focuses on identifying hidden structure within complex systems in order to develop tractable approximation methods and interpretable algorithmic approaches.

Methodologically, I am particularly interested in approximation algorithms, decomposition techniques, computational complexity, and scalable frameworks for operational decision-making under uncertainty.

# DESIGN PHILOSOPHY NOTES FOR CODEX

The website should:
- feel modern and professional,
- avoid academic job-market aesthetics,
- avoid excessive visual clutter,
- avoid corporate startup aesthetics,
- avoid flashy animations,
- and emphasize hierarchy, whitespace, and typography.

The visual style should communicate:
- rigor,
- calm confidence,
- intellectual clarity,
- and structure.

The site should feel more like:
- a carefully curated faculty profile,
than:
- a graduate student portfolio.

Preferred characteristics:
- clean typography,
- restrained color palette,
- clear section hierarchy,
- generous whitespace,
- subtle interactions only,
- elegant scrolling,
- and readable layout.

The website should prioritize:
- readability,
- intellectual coherence,
- and fast signal extraction for academic audiences.

Target audience priority:
1. External letter writers
2. OR/MSOM researchers
3. Promotion/reappointment committee
4. Potential collaborators
5. Conference researchers
6. Industry researchers
